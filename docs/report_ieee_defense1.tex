\begin{abstract}
This report presents the first-stage development of an autonomous wearable pendant that simulates interactive fluid dynamics on a custom circular matrix of 240 LEDs based on an STM32 microcontroller. The device measures physical tilt and acceleration via an ultra-low-power 6-axis IMU and visualizes sloshing fluid waves in real time. We present the project aims, system architecture, component selection, and a fully validated C++17 Position-Based Fluids (PBF) simulation core. The engine requires only 5.95\,KB of static RAM with zero dynamic heap allocations ($O(1)$ memory) and executes in 0.010\,ms per step ($>100,000$\,FPS on PC, $\approx 1\%$ estimated CPU load on an 84\,MHz ARM Cortex-M4 at 40\,FPS). Furthermore, drawing from direct consultation and hardware handover from the previous year's team, we establish a Charlieplexing display topology (16 GPIOs driving 240 discrete 0603 LEDs), an empirical power budget ($\approx 6.2$ hours on a 300\,mAh Li-Po cell), a complete Bill of Materials, and a manufacturing roadmap toward custom PCB fabrication for Defense 2.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
wearable robotics, fluid simulation, STM32, Charlieplexing, IMU sensor, Position-Based Dynamics, embedded systems

\section{Introduction}
Interactive wearable accessories and fidget devices offer unique visual feedback, yet existing electronic pendants are predominantly limited to static patterns or pre-recorded animations. Creating real-time physical liquid sloshing inside an ultra-compact wearable enclosure presents significant embedded engineering challenges across computational fluid dynamics, pin-constrained display multiplexing, and power optimization.

The previous year's student team developed an initial proof-of-concept; however, their architecture relied on a modular stack with a developer board mounted externally via wiring harnesses, resulting in a bulky enclosure ($\approx 8\text{--}10$\,cm) unsuitable for practical daily wear. The primary objective of this project is full miniaturization into an autonomous, self-contained circular pendant ($\varnothing 45$\,mm, 11.5\,mm depth) hosting an integrated custom PCB, an onboard microcontroller, and a rechargeable battery subsystem.

\section{Aim and Objectives}
\textbf{Aim.} To design and build an autonomous, compact wearable electronic pendant that simulates realistic fluid sloshing dynamics on a circular 240-LED matrix responding to user motion.

\textbf{Objectives.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Implement and benchmark an embedded C++ physics engine capable of real-time sloshing simulation within $\le 64$\,KB of microcontroller SRAM;
    \item Drive a high-density circular array of 240 blue LEDs using a Charlieplexing topology constrained to 16 GPIO lines;
    \item Read physical acceleration and tilt vectors at $\ge 100$\,Hz using an ultra-low-power 6-axis IMU via I2C;
    \item Design an internal two-sided circular PCB ($\varnothing 42$\,mm) and a lightweight enclosure ($\varnothing 45$\,mm, 11.5\,mm depth) with an optical diffuser;
    \item Ensure $\ge 5$ hours of autonomous battery life from a 300\,mAh Li-Po cell rechargeable via USB Type-C;
    \item Validate the complete physical pipeline on desktop and developer hardware before custom PCB fabrication.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture and Chosen Technologies}

\textbf{Microcontroller Strategy.} We adopt a two-stage development methodology:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Prototyping \& Bring-Up Platform:} We adopt a NUCLEO-L432KC board (ARM Cortex-M4 @ 80\,MHz, 64\,KB SRAM) inherited from the previous team as our benchtop bring-up platform for IMU interface verification and timer profiling prior to custom PCB fabrication.
    \item \emph{Custom PCB Integration (Final Stage):} The standalone STM32F401CCU6 in a compact UFQFPN48 package ($7 \times 7$\,mm) is selected. It features an ARM Cortex-M4 core at 84\,MHz with a hardware single-precision Floating-Point Unit (FPU), 256\,KB Flash, 64\,KB SRAM, and 36 available GPIOs. Firmware is flashed and debugged over a 4-pin Serial Wire Debug (SWD) interface via an external ST-Link V2 dongle.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Display Architecture: Charlieplexing.} Driving 240 discrete LEDs directly would exceed both microcontroller pin counts and routing channel space. We implement Charlieplexing, leveraging tri-state logic (\texttt{HIGH}, \texttt{LOW}, and High-Impedance \texttt{Hi-Z}). With $N$ GPIO lines, the maximum addressable LED count is:
\begin{equation}
    L_{\text{max}} = N(N - 1)
\end{equation}
Allocating $N = 16$ pins yields exactly:
\begin{equation}
    16 \times (16 - 1) = 240 \text{ LEDs}
\end{equation}
Pairs of LEDs are connected in anti-parallel configurations across pin pairs. At any instant, one pin drives \texttt{HIGH}, one drives \texttt{LOW}, and the remaining 14 lines are set to \texttt{Hi-Z}. Scanning at $>100$\,Hz provides flicker-free persistence of vision.

\emph{Rejection of Addressable LEDs}: Smart RGB LEDs (e.g., WS2812B-2020) were formally evaluated and rejected: each integrated IC consumes $\approx 1$\,mA quiescent current even when unlit, totaling $\approx 240$\,mA idle draw for 240 pixels. This would deplete a 300\,mAh battery in under 50 minutes without active illumination. In contrast, discrete SMD 0603 LEDs in a Charlieplexed scanning matrix draw an average of only $\approx 25$\,mA. Current-limiting resistors on each of the 16 driving lines safeguard the microcontroller I/O ports.

\textbf{Motion Sensing.} A 6-axis LSM6DS3TR-C IMU communicates over I2C at 400\,kHz. With an operating current of only 0.9\,mA, it samples linear acceleration and gravity vectors at 104\,Hz to orient the simulation frame.

\section{Work Done So Far: Fluid Physics Core}

\subsection{Algorithm Selection}
To simulate realistic liquid on an embedded MCU with only 64\,KB SRAM, three computational methodologies were evaluated:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Eulerian Grid Navier-Stokes}: Requires large 2D velocity and pressure matrices alongside iterative linear Poisson solvers, exceeding internal SRAM and execution budgets.
    \item \emph{Cellular Automata (Falling Sand)}: Extremely lightweight, but models discrete granular displacement without momentum, vortices, or surface sloshing waves.
    \item \emph{Position-Based Fluids (Our Choice)}: Represents fluid as $N$ discrete particles governed by geometric distance constraints. It guarantees unconditional numerical stability, zero heap allocations, and microsecond-scale execution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Governing Equations}
The fluid volume is discretized into $N$ particles with positions $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and velocities $\mathbf{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ inside a normalized circular boundary of radius $R = 1.0$. Each discrete simulation step $\Delta t$ proceeds through four stages:

1) \emph{Position Prediction:} Particles advance under current velocity and external gravity acceleration $\mathbf{g}$ measured by the IMU:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{v}_i^* &= \alpha \cdot \mathbf{v}_i + \mathbf{g} \Delta t \\
    \mathbf{x}_i^* &= \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{v}_i^* \Delta t
\end{align}
where $\alpha = 0.988$ provides natural atmospheric velocity damping.

2) \emph{Incompressibility Constraints:} Liquid volume conservation is enforced through pairwise non-penetration constraints. When distance $d_{ij} = |\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_j^*|$ falls below particle diameter $2r$, particles are separated along contact normal $\mathbf{n}_{ij}$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{x}_i = \frac{1}{2} k \, (2r - d_{ij}) \, \mathbf{n}_{ij}, \quad \Delta \mathbf{x}_j = -\Delta \mathbf{x}_i
\end{equation}
where $k = 0.92$ denotes constraint relaxation stiffness.

3) \emph{Boundary Clamping:} Particles are confined within the circular cavity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_i^* = R_{\text{max}} \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^*}{|\mathbf{x}_i^*|}, \quad \text{if } |\mathbf{x}_i^*| > R_{\text{max}}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\text{max}} = 0.97$.

4) \emph{Velocity Update from Displacement:} Rather than integrating stiff pressure forces, velocity is derived directly from actual positional displacement:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_i = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_i}{\Delta t}
\end{equation}
When particles collide with the curved boundary, non-penetration forces displace them laterally. Because velocity is updated from this displacement, downward impacts naturally produce horizontal splash waves and surface sloshing.

5) \emph{Viscosity Filtering (XSPH):} An artificial viscosity filter smooths neighboring velocity vectors, maintaining cohesive droplet flow:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{v}_i = c \sum_{j} (\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i) \left(1 - \frac{d_{ij}}{h}\right)
\end{equation}
where $c = 0.015$ is the viscosity coefficient and $h$ is the smoothing radius.

\section{Planned Implementation and Fabrication}

\textbf{Surface-Mount Assembly Strategy.} Drawing from the empirical feedback of the previous year's team, manual hand-soldering of 240 discrete 0603 SMD components using a conventional soldering iron was identified as a critical point of failure due to thermal stress, component misalignment, and high solder-bridge defect rates. To ensure manufacturing repeatability and electrical integrity, the assembly process employs laser-cut stainless steel stencils, lead-free solder paste, and controlled hot-air convective reflow.

\textbf{PCB Architecture.} A custom 2-layer circular PCB ($\varnothing 42$\,mm) is routed in KiCad. The front layer hosts exclusively the 240-LED matrix, while the reverse side accommodates the STM32F401 microcontroller, LSM6DS3 IMU, power regulation circuitry, and 4-pad SWD programming interface. To account for fabrication and assembly yield, five boards are ordered to secure at least two fully operational units.

\textbf{Enclosure and Optical Diffuser.} The enclosure is modeled in Fusion 360 as a two-piece circular case ($\varnothing 45$\,mm outer diameter, 11.5\,mm depth) containing internal snap-fits, a Type-C cutout, and a 30$\times$20$\times$5\,mm battery pocket. Prototypes will be 3D-printed in high-resolution resin. A semi-translucent frosted acrylic diffuser front cover blends discrete LED points into a continuous liquid meniscus.
