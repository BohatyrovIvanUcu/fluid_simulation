\begin{abstract}
This report outlines the engineering design, physical principles, and embedded architecture for a wearable autonomous smart pendant that simulates interactive fluid dynamics on a custom circular 240-LED matrix. We provide a rigorous evaluation of computational fluid dynamics methodologies suitable for resource-constrained ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers. We present a validated C++17 Position-Based Fluids (PBF) core with XSPH viscosity requiring only 5.95~KB of SRAM with zero dynamic allocations ($O(1)$ heap), executing in 0.024~ms per frame ($>40,000$~FPS) on standard processors. Furthermore, this document specifies the hardware architecture based on Charlieplexing (driving 240 LEDs with 16 GPIO pins), power estimation for $\ge 5$ hours of battery life, component selection rationale, and project milestones towards Defense 2.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Embedded systems, fluid simulation, Position-Based Fluids (PBF), Charlieplexing, STM32, inertial measurement unit, wearable robotics.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Fluid Simulation: Mathematical Formulation and Algorithm Selection}

\subsection{Comparison of Theoretical Approaches}
Simulating convincing fluid motion on a resource-constrained microcontroller (ARM Cortex-M with $32$--$64$\,KB of RAM) requires careful algorithmic tradeoffs. Four paradigms were mathematically evaluated:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Eulerian Grid Methods (Navier-Stokes / Stable Fluids)}: Discretizes space into a fixed grid, solving semi-Lagrangian advection and a Poisson pressure equation:
    \begin{equation}
        \nabla^2 p = \frac{\rho_0}{\Delta t} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}^*
    \end{equation}
    \textit{Rejection Rationale}: Requires solving large linear sparse systems (Jacobi/Gauss-Seidel iterations) and storing multiple 2D scalar/vector fields ($u, v, p$). On a $18 \times 18$ grid, this demands excessive memory bandwidth and floating-point operations beyond the real-time budget of microcontrollers.

    \item \textbf{Cellular Automata (CA / Falling Sand)}: Discretizes fluid into binary occupancy states evaluated with local neighborhood transition rules.
    \textit{Rejection Rationale}: Fails to model true continuous momentum conservation, centripetal vortices, and wave sloshing. Under motion, the fluid behaves like dry sand or powder rather than a cohesive liquid.

    \item \textbf{Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH)}: An explicit Lagrangian particle formulation evaluating density through smoothing kernels and state equations (Tait's Equation):
    \begin{equation}
        p = B \left( \left(\frac{\rho}{\rho_0}\right)^\gamma - 1 \right)
    \end{equation}
    \textit{Rejection Rationale}: Highly stiff and sensitive to the time step $\Delta t$. SPH suffers from tensile instability (particle clumping) and requires microsecond sub-stepping to avoid numerical explosions, rendering it inefficient for low-frequency MCU loops.

    \item \textbf{Position-Based Fluids (PBF) with XSPH (Selected Method)}: Formulates fluid incompressibility as geometric distance constraints resolved directly on particle positions (Macklin \& M{\"u}ller, 2013), coupled with an XSPH velocity filter.
    \textit{Selection Rationale}: 100\% unconditionally stable, zero dynamic memory allocations ($O(1)$ heap), preserves momentum and inertial vortices, and executes within microseconds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation and Governing Equations}
The fluid is discretized into $N$ particles with coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and velocities $\mathbf{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ inside a circular cavity of normalized radius $R = 1.0$.

\subsubsection{1. Force Integration and Position Prediction}
At each discrete time step $\Delta t$, particle positions are predicted under external acceleration (gravity vector $\mathbf{g}$ from the IMU) and rotational container drag:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{v}_i^* &= \alpha_{\text{damp}} \mathbf{v}_i + \mathbf{g} \Delta t + \mathbf{a}_{\text{rot}} \\
    \mathbf{x}_i^* &= \mathbf{x}_i + \mathbf{v}_i^* \Delta t
\end{align}
where $\alpha_{\text{damp}} = 0.988$ is the velocity retention factor. Rotational coupling from pendant angular velocity $\omega$ imparts tangential boundary velocity $\mathbf{v}_{\text{wall}} = (\omega y_i, -\omega x_i)$.

\subsubsection{2. Incompressibility and Contact Constraints}
Volume conservation and fluid incompressibility are enforced through pairwise non-penetration distance constraints between neighboring particles:
\begin{equation}
    C(\mathbf{x}_i^*, \mathbf{x}_j^*) = |\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_j^*| - 2r_{\text{part}} \ge 0
\end{equation}
When particles overlap ($d_{ij} = |\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_j^*| < 2r_{\text{part}}$), position corrections are applied along the contact normal $\mathbf{n}_{ij} = (\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_j^*) / d_{ij}$:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \mathbf{x}_i = \frac{1}{2} k_{\text{rep}} (2r_{\text{part}} - d_{ij}) \mathbf{n}_{ij}, \quad \Delta \mathbf{x}_j = -\Delta \mathbf{x}_i
\end{equation}
where $k_{\text{rep}} = 0.92$ is the relaxation stiffness parameter.

\subsubsection{3. Boundary Enclosure Constraint}
Particles are strictly constrained inside the circular pendant cavity:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_i^* = R_{\text{max}} \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^*}{|\mathbf{x}_i^*|}, \quad \text{if } |\mathbf{x}_i^*| > R_{\text{max}}
\end{equation}
with boundary threshold $R_{\text{max}} = 0.97$.

\subsubsection{4. Canonical Velocity Derivation}
In accordance with PBF theory, particle velocities are derived directly from the net position displacement produced by the constraint solver:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_i = \frac{\mathbf{x}_i^* - \mathbf{x}_i}{\Delta t}
\end{equation}
This formulation naturally converts downward kinetic impact into rapid lateral spreading (splash waves) when colliding with the container floor, while guaranteeing that static fluid in equilibrium converges to $\mathbf{v}_i \to 0$ without artificial energy pumping.

\subsubsection{5. XSPH Artificial Viscosity}
To model laminar shear coupling and prevent particle fragmentation, a post-processing XSPH velocity filter is evaluated:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_i \leftarrow \mathbf{v}_i + c \sum_{j} (\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i) W(\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j, h)
\end{equation}
where $c = 0.015$ is the calibrated viscosity constant, and $W(r, h) = 1 - \frac{r}{h}$ is the smoothing kernel over interaction radius $h = 2.2 r_{\text{part}}$.
